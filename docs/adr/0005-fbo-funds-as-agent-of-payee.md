# The platform holds merchant funds in an FBO account as agent of payee

Plumb has three parties: the platform collects invoice payments from payers on behalf of merchants and earns a fee on each one. We model the arrangement where the platform holds merchant money itself. A simulated PSP acquires card payments and settles them into an FBO custodial account at a sponsor bank, ACH debits land there too, and payouts go back out over ACH. The platform acts as each merchant's **agent of payee** under a written merchant agreement, working within FinCEN's payment processor exemption (31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5)(ii)(B): goods and services, a formal agreement with the merchant, funds in and out only through bank-only rails) and the agent-of-payee exemption in the CSBS Money Transmission Modernization Act (§3.01(b)). We chose this because the ledger, reconciliation and payout lifecycle are what Plumb exists to show, and only an arrangement where the platform holds the money needs them. **This is a design assumption, not a legal conclusion:** a real launch would need state-by-state counsel. Sources are in the [FBO research](https://github.com/stvijayanand/plumb/blob/research/fbo-money-transmission/docs/research/fbo-money-transmission.md).

## Considered options

- **Stripe Connect (the "buy" option).** A licensed money transmitter (Stripe Payments Company) holds the balances and runs payouts. This is what a real early-stage company would probably pick, and the design doc should say so. Rejected for Plumb because it hides the ledger, settlement and payouts behind Stripe's API.
- **Registered payment facilitator under an acquirer.** Adds card-network registration, monitoring of sponsored merchants and acquirer liability. It's real work but beside the point here, and it still receives settlement into an FBO account, so it changes nothing about who holds the funds. Named as a later-stage option.
- **Merchant money in the platform's operating account.** Rejected: it mixes merchant money with the platform's own (commingling), gets no FDIC pass-through insurance, and breaks the custodial framing that both exemptions rely on.
- **Money transmitter licenses instead of exemptions.** Rejected: state-by-state licensing is costly and slow, and has nothing to do with what the project is meant to show.

## Consequences

- **A payer has paid at capture.** A card capture or ACH `succeeded` emits the event the billing platform uses to mark the invoice paid; invoice state belongs to the billing platform, not Plumb. Settlement moves merchant money from unsettled to available and never changes invoice state. Payer-side reversals (an ACH return, a simulated chargeback) emit an event that may reopen the invoice; refunds emit an event and any credit note is billing's concern. **The platform failing to pay a merchant (a payout return, a payout hold, the platform going insolvent) never reopens an invoice.**
- **The FBO account holds only merchant money.** The margin sweep moves the platform's share out at each settlement. The risk reserve stays in, because it is merchant money held back ([chart of accounts](https://github.com/stvijayanand/plumb/issues/3)).
- **No merchant is ever funded by another merchant's money.** The FBO account must always hold at least the sum of each merchant's positive balance, so the platform covers any negative merchant balance from operating cash. When the top-up posts, and how the money is recovered from the merchant, belong to [ADR: Negative-balance policy for merchants](https://github.com/stvijayanand/plumb/issues/6).
- **Two FBO checks.** (1) A ledger check: FBO cash = Σ(available + risk reserve + payouts in transit) across merchants, which confirms the postings are correct. Unsettled money is still with the PSP, so it isn't part of this sum. (2) A daily check of the ledger's FBO cash against a balance statement from the simulated sponsor bank, which catches money the ledger never saw. The bank-rail simulator therefore also produces a daily FBO balance file. The file format and how breaks are classified belong to the reconciliation design.
- **The per-merchant accounts record who owns each dollar.** FDIC pass-through insurance relies on that record, and so would the FDIC's September 2024 proposal for daily per-owner reconciliation of custodial accounts (89 FR 80135; whether it has been finalized is unverified). Plumb's design meets it either way.
- **Payouts go only over ACH to the merchant's own bank account on file.** No payments to third parties, cheques, wallets, stored value or instant payouts: paying out outside bank-only rails breaks the processor exemption. Merchant onboarding, KYB and bank-account verification are out of scope; the account comes from seed data and is treated as verified.
