Plumb payment state machine: card and ACH

Question: what states does a payment go through, which moves are legal, and how does one machine survive the awkward cases: a PSP timeout whose outcome is unknown, duplicate and out-of-order webhooks, review holds with an SLA, partial capture, refunds that fail after succeeding, retries after a decline, and ACH, which has no authorization and can be returned days after it succeeded?

The proposal to react to: one payment record, two transition tables (card and ACH). Things Plumb or a person asks for (commands) are rejected when illegal. Things the PSP or bank reports (PSP facts) can't be rejected, because the money already moved. A fact is either applied, ignored as a duplicate or late arrival when the payment has already been through that state, or raised as a reconciliation break when it contradicts what Plumb knows. Refunds are their own small records, and settlement is an amount on the payment, not a state.

Assumed, not decided here: the risk check runs before authorization (the synchronous-vs-async ADR is still open); what happens when the risk service doesn't answer (fail-open vs fail-closed) isn't modelled. Every payment is $1,000. A payment gets at most 3 attempts.
